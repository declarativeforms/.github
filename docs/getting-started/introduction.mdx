## What is Declarative Forms?

Declarative Forms is a developer-first alternative to traditional form platforms. Instead of building forms through visual editors, you define how a form works in YAML and the platform renders the experience for you.

It is designed for teams that need more than a quick standalone form. When forms become part of an operational workflow or product experience, teams usually want a more repeatable way to define them, keep them consistent, and evolve them over time.

---

## Forms as Code

A form is a YAML file. You describe the structure, content, and flow, and the platform handles the rendering and runtime experience.

```yaml
version: 1
title: "Feedback"
description: "Help us improve our product!"

locale: "en"
measurements:
  # ...

start_date: "2026-01-01"
end_date: "2026-03-31"

completion:
    # ...

sections:
  - id: section_1
    title: "Section 1"
    fields:
      - id: question_1
        type: email
        label: What’s your email?
        placeholder: hello@declarativeforms.com
        validators:
          - required
    next: done

connections:
    # ...
```

## Why code instead of a visual editor

Visual editors are a good fit when you need to publish a simple form quickly. But when forms become part of a product or an operational workflow, the model you use to define them starts to matter.

Declarative Forms is built for teams that want a more consistent and repeatable way to manage forms, especially when there are many of them or when they need to fit into a broader system. Compared with tools like Typeform, Fillout, Google Forms, or JotForm, the tradeoff is simple: visual builders optimize for speed and convenience, while Declarative Forms optimizes for control over how forms are defined and maintained.

Git can support that workflow through version history and collaboration, but it is not the main reason to use the product. The main reason is to give teams a more structured way to define forms when those forms are no longer just one-off pages.

## Best fit

Declarative Forms is a strong fit when:

- you manage multiple forms and want them to follow a consistent model
- forms are part of an operational workflow or product experience
- your team prefers a structured, repeatable way to define and maintain forms

## Not the best fit

Visual form builders are often a better fit when:

- you need to launch a one-off form as quickly as possible
- the main priority is non-technical setup over long-term consistency
- your workflow depends more on editor convenience than on a defined form model
